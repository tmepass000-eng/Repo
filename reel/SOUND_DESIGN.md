## 3. Sound design palette

- **Sub impacts:** sine sweep 90→42 Hz, pitch envelope 60 ms, soft clip via saturation, mono below 120 Hz.
- **Metallic transients:** sampled titanium / brushed steel knocks, pitched ±3 semitones per use, short
  plate reverb (0.6 s).
- **Designed whooshes:** filtered noise with band-pass sweep 400 Hz→6 kHz, pan automation following camera.
- **Liquid:** close-mic droplets, layered with granular water texture; droplets pitched up for premium feel.
- **Interface:** clean sine ticks 1.5–3 kHz, extremely short (8 ms), plus soft UI confirmations.
- **Granular textures:** stretched glass/ice recordings at 400% for the mist and procedural sections.
- **Reverse risers:** reverse-reverb swells of the track itself, 1.5–2 s, ending exactly on the cut.
- **Atmosphere:** low, wide pad at −28 dB for continuity across cuts.

## 4. Mix

- Loudness target: −14 LUFS integrated; true peak ≤ −1.0 dBTP; no limiter pumping.
- Bass management: high-pass everything except sub/impact layers at 100 Hz; mono below 120 Hz.
- Effects sit 3–6 dB under music except the hero transients at 00:00.35, 00:11.00, 00:22.00 and 00:26.20,
  which may briefly lead.
- De-ess and tame 6–8 kHz on shimmer/sparkle layers to avoid harshness on phone speakers.
- Check the mix on a phone speaker: the sub impacts must still read as rhythm, not as mud.
- Dialogue-free film → no sidechain needed; use volume automation for the three silence gaps.

## 5. Licensing

All music and effects must be either original synthesis/recording by the creator or covered by a
commercial license that permits paid advertising use and platform distribution (e.g. an owned
subscription tier that explicitly grants commercial/ads rights). Keep the license receipt and project
file with the delivery package. No "trending audio" — it cannot be licensed for brand advertising.
